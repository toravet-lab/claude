## SECTION A: WORKLOAD PUSHBACK (12 SCRIPTS)

### When Someone's Asking You to Take On More Work

**Script A1 - Direct and Honest**
"I'm at capacity right now. If you want me to take this on, what should I deprioritize?"

*When: Someone's asking for something new while you're already full. Works with peers, managers, and stakeholders.*
*Tone: Matter-of-fact. Not apologetic.*
*Impact: Forces them to make the trade-off decision.*

**Script A2 - With Quality Concern**
"I want to make sure I do this well—what's the timeline? And if it's urgent, what on my current list should move?"

*When: You care about quality and don't want to shortchange anything.*
*Tone: Collaborative, solution-oriented.*
*Impact: Shows you're thinking about outcomes, not just refusing.*

**Script A3 - Asking for Clarity**
"Help me understand the priority here—where does this fall relative to X and Y?"

*When: You're not sure if it's actually high-priority.*
*Tone: Curious, not defensive.*
*Impact: Often they realize it's not that important once they have to compare.*

**Script A4 - Offering a Real Timeline**
"I can take this on, but it will push back [X]. Does that still make sense?"

*When: You could do it, just not immediately.*
*Tone: Realistic, helpful.*
*Impact: You're saying yes—just with an honest timeline.*

**Script A5 - To Your Boss**
"I want to make sure I'm focused on the highest-impact work. Can we go through my list and you tell me what takes priority?"

*When: You need to align on what matters most.*
*Tone: Eager to do the right thing.*
*Impact: Puts priority-setting on your manager where it belongs.*

**Script A6 - Redirecting to Someone Else**
"I'm slammed right now, but [person] might have capacity. Want me to introduce you?"

*When: You genuinely can't do it, but someone else can.*
*Tone: Helpful, not dismissive.*
*Impact: You're solving their problem, not refusing it.*

**Script A7 - The Conditional Yes**
"I can take this on if [condition]. Does that work for you?"

*When: You're willing, but only if something changes.*
*Example conditions: "if the deadline moves to Friday," "if you provide the data," "if we reduce scope."*
*Tone: Collaborative.*
*Impact: You're saying yes with an explicit condition.*

**Script A8 - With Risk Acknowledgment**
"I can do this, but I want to be transparent—adding this will increase risk on [other project]. Are you okay with that trade-off?"

*When: You want them to understand the actual cost.*
*Tone: Professional, factual.*
*Impact: They make an informed decision.*

**Script A9 - Pushing Back on "Quick" Asks**
"That sounds quick, but it'll take me [realistic time]. Is it worth that?"

*When: Someone says "quick ask" but it's not actually quick.*
*Tone: Honest, without attitude.*
*Impact: Sets realistic expectations.*

**Script A10 - The Delay**
"I can't take this on right now, but I have bandwidth [specific date/timeframe]. Would that work?"

*When: You'll have capacity, just not immediately.*
*Tone: Helpful, future-focused.*
*Impact: You're not refusing—just timing it realistically.*

**Script A11 - Making Them Think**
"That's interesting. Before I commit, can you help me understand why this is important and how it connects to our goals?"

*When: You're genuinely uncertain if it deserves a yes.*
*Tone: Thoughtful.*
*Impact: Forces them to justify the ask. Many won't be able to.*

**Script A12 - The Firm No**
"I'm not able to take that on right now. I need to stay focused on [current priority]."

*When: You need to be clear and done with the conversation.*
*Tone: Calm, professional, final.*
*Impact: No ambiguity. No room for negotiation.*

## SECTION B: DEADLINE NEGOTIATION (10 SCRIPTS)

### When Someone Wants Something by an Unrealistic Timeline

**Script B1 - State Reality**
"Given everything on my plate, the earliest I can have this ready is [date]."

*When: The deadline they want is just not realistic.*
*Tone: Factual, not apologetic.*
*Impact: You're not refusing—you're giving them the real timeline.*

**Script B2 - Offer a Choice**
"I can get you a rough draft by Friday, or a polished version by Monday. Which helps more?"

*When: You're willing to move, but there's a trade-off between speed and quality.*
*Tone: Practical, helpful.*
*Impact: They choose, based on what they actually need.*

**Script B3 - Quality vs. Speed**
"I want to make sure this is done right. Rushing this would mean cutting corners on [specific thing]. Is that okay?"

*When: You want them to understand what speed actually costs.*
*Tone: Professional concern.*
*Impact: Often they'll decide the deadline wasn't as firm as they thought.*

**Script B4 - Question the Urgency**
"What's driving the urgency? Is it truly needed by [date], or does it just feel urgent?"

*When: You suspect the deadline might be arbitrary.*
*Tone: Curious, not challenging.*
*Impact: Gives them room to adjust without losing face.*

**Script B5 - Conditional Yes**
"I can have it by Wednesday if you can get me the final requirements by EOD Monday."

*When: You can meet the deadline, but they need to do their part.*
*Tone: Collaborative.*
*Impact: Shifts the dependency. Shows it's a two-way street.*

**Script B6 - With Competing Priorities**
"I can hit that deadline, but it means I'll have to pause work on [X]. Is that what you want?"

*When: It's possible, but creates visible trade-offs.*
*Tone: Neutral, informative.*
*Impact: Makes the cost explicit.*

**Script B7 - Asking for Help**
"I can try to hit that timeline, but it's going to be tight. Is there any way to get additional resources or support?"

*When: It's possible, but you'd need help.*
*Tone: Problem-solving.*
*Impact: You're saying yes, but asking for what you need.*

**Script B8 - Breaking It Into Pieces**
"I can deliver the core by [date], and the rest by [later date]. Does that work?"

*When: Part of it could be done faster, and part could follow.*
*Tone: Solution-oriented.*
*Impact: You're delivering value faster, just not all at once.*

**Script B9 - Setting Expectations**
"If I'm going to deliver this by [aggressive date], I need to set expectations—it won't be perfect, but it will be done."

*When: You can do it, but need them to understand the quality trade-off.*
*Tone: Direct, honest.*
*Impact: No surprises later.*

**Script B10 - The Firm Deadline**
"I cannot deliver this by [their date]. I can have it by [your date]. That's the realistic timeline."

*When: You need to be firm because the deadline is genuinely impossible.*
*Tone: Final, not negotiable.*
*Impact: Clear boundary. Removes ambiguity.*

## SECTION C: SCOPE CREEP (10 SCRIPTS)

### When a Project Keeps Expanding and You Never Agreed to That

**Script C1 - Call It Out**
"That sounds like an expansion of the original scope. Should we revisit the timeline?"

*When: They're adding things mid-project.*
*Tone: Neutral, factual.*
*Impact: Names what's happening without being accusatory.*

**Script C2 - Phase It**
"Happy to include that—should we treat it as a phase two, or fold it into the original plan? If we fold it in, the timeline will shift."

*When: The addition is worthwhile, but needs to be planned.*
*Tone: Collaborative, professional.*
*Impact: You're saying yes to the work, just planning it properly.*

**Script C3 - Ask for Trade-Offs**
"Adding that will push out the deadline [or require more resources]. What's your priority—speed, scope, or resources?"

*When: You need them to understand that something has to give.*
*Tone: Factual, solution-oriented.*
*Impact: Forces a real decision.*

**Script C4 - Define Done First**
"Before we add more, can we define what 'done' looks like for the original scope? That way we know when we're actually finished."

*When: You want to prevent endless expansion.*
*Tone: Reasonable, professional.*
*Impact: Creates a finish line.*

**Script C5 - Redirect to Decision-Maker**
"That's a good idea—should we flag it for [manager/stakeholder] to decide if it fits the original goals?"

*When: You want to remove yourself from the decision.*
*Tone: Helpful, collaborative.*
*Impact: Moves the decision up the chain where it belongs.*

**Script C6 - Transparency**
"Here's what I'm seeing: the original scope was X, and we've added Y and Z. That's roughly [% increase] more work. Do we all agree on that?"

*When: You want to make the creep visible.*
*Tone: Matter-of-fact.*
*Impact: Often people realize how much scope has grown once it's quantified.*

**Script C7 - With Capacity Language**
"I want to be honest about capacity. With the original scope, I can deliver [date]. Adding this would extend it to [new date]."

*When: You want to frame it as a capacity issue, not a refusal.*
*Tone: Professional, honest.*
*Impact: Shows you're thinking about realistic delivery.*

**Script C8 - Asking for Resources**
"I'm happy to include that. To do the original scope + this addition well, I'd need help. Can we bring in [resource]?"

*When: You want to say yes but need support.*
*Tone: Solution-focused.*
*Impact: You're saying yes, just asking for what you need.*

**Script C9 - Quality Warning**
"If we add this, something on the original list will take a hit. Are you okay with that, or should we prioritize?"

*When: You want to make the quality trade-off explicit.*
*Tone: Concerned but professional.*
*Impact: They understand the real cost.*

**Script C10 - The Documentation Play**
"I want to confirm our scope in writing—the original deliverables were [list]. Is that what we're still shooting for, or have those changed?"

*When: You want to document what was agreed.*
*Tone: Helpful, professional.*
*Impact: Creates a record. Prevents future disputes.*

## SECTION D: MEETING BOUNDARIES (10 SCRIPTS)

### When You're Invited to Meetings You Don't Need to Attend

**Script D1 - Direct and Helpful**
"I don't think I'm needed for this one—can you send me the notes if anything affects my work?"

*When: You're definitely not essential.*
*Tone: Helpful, not dismissive.*
*Impact: Shows you care about outcomes, not just being present.*

**Script D2 - Offering an Alternative**
"I probably don't have much to contribute here, but feel free to DM me with questions. Happy to jump on a quick call if something comes up."

*When: You might have value, but not for the whole meeting.*
*Tone: Collaborative, accessible.*
*Impact: You're protecting time while staying available.*

**Script D3 - Questioning Your Presence**
"I want to make sure I'm not wasting time—am I actually needed here?"

*When: You're genuinely uncertain why you were added.*
*Tone: Curious, not accusatory.*
*Impact: Puts the burden on them to justify.*

**Script D4 - Async Option**
"Could this be handled async? I could read a summary instead of attending live."

*When: The information would be useful, but doesn't need real-time discussion.*
*Tone: Problem-solving.*
*Impact: You get the information, keep your time.*

**Script D5 - For Recurring Meetings**
"I've been on this recurring meeting for a while. Should I still be attending?"

*When: It's a standing meeting and you're not sure about your role.*
*Tone: Neutral, practical.*
*Impact: Often they'll say "no, thanks for asking" and you're off.*

**Script D6 - With Respect**
"This sounds important, but I don't think I'm the right audience. Have you thought about who really needs to be there?"

*When: You want to be helpful about who actually should attend.*
*Tone: Collaborative.*
*Impact: Might improve the meeting even if you leave.*

**Script D7 - Capacity-Based**
"I'm running over capacity this week—if this isn't essential, I'd like to sit it out. Fill me in if it affects my work?"

*When: You're genuinely slammed.*
*Tone: Honest, specific.*
*Impact: Shows it's about priorities, not difficulty.*

**Script D8 - For Status Updates**
"For status updates, I'm happy to send a written update instead of attending. Would that work?"

*When: The meeting is just information-sharing.*
*Tone: Offering a solution.*
*Impact: They get the information, you keep your time.*

**Script D9 - For Decision Meetings**
"I trust you to make the right decision here. I don't need to weigh in—just let me know the outcome."

*When: They've included you but don't actually need your input.*
*Tone: Confident, respectful.*
*Impact: Removes you while showing confidence in the decision-maker.*

**Script D10 - The Clear No**
"I'm not able to make that meeting. If you need my input, can we find a 15-min call this week instead?"

*When: You simply can't attend and are offering an alternative.*
*Tone: Professional, solution-oriented.*
*Impact: You're not just refusing—you're offering what you can do.*

## SECTION E: SLACK / "QUICK ASK" DEFENSE (10 SCRIPTS)

### When Someone's Interrupting Your Work with Non-Urgent Requests

**Script E1 - Buy Time**
"I'm in the middle of something focused—can I circle back to this at [time]?"

*When: It's not urgent and you need to protect focus.*
*Tone: Helpful, not dismissive.*
*Impact: You're not refusing—you're setting a timeline.*

**Script E2 - Require Context**
"Can you drop this in a ticket/email so I can prioritize it properly with everything else?"

*When: You want something more formal than a chat.*
*Tone: Practical.*
*Impact: Creates a record and lets you batch your response.*

**Script E3 - Set an Expectation**
"I check Slack in batches—usually around 11 AM and 4 PM. I'll get back to you then. For anything urgent, use [phone/email]."

*When: You want to set a pattern of less interruption.*
*Tone: Friendly but clear.*
*Impact: Over time, people stop expecting immediate responses.*

**Script E4 - If It's Constant**
"I'm getting a lot of these interruptions—can we find a better system for non-urgent asks?"

*When: The pattern is the problem, not any one request.*
*Tone: Collaborative, problem-solving.*
*Impact: Addresses the systemic issue, not just one interruption.*

**Script E5 - Helpful Redirection**
"I'm not the right person for this—have you tried [person]?"

*When: It's someone else's domain.*
*Tone: Helpful, not dismissive.*
*Impact: You're solving their problem, just not doing the work yourself.*

**Script E6 - Polite Decline**
"I'm not able to jump in on this right now—let me know if it's still needed later."

*When: It's optional and you don't have bandwidth.*
*Tone: Professional, brief.*
*Impact: Clear boundary.*

**Script E7 - With Respect for Their Time**
"I appreciate you reaching out. I don't have capacity for this right now, but I wanted to let you know quickly so you can ask someone else."

*When: They're waiting for a response and you want to be respectful of their time.*
*Tone: Considerate.*
*Impact: You're saying no, but in a way that respects their deadline.*

**Script E8 - Offering Async**
"I don't have time for a real-time conversation, but I can read written context. Can you send that over?"

*When: They might need to talk it through, but you're offering a written alternative.*
*Tone: Willing to help, just on your terms.*
*Impact: You're still being helpful, just not synchronously.*

**Script E9 - Time-Boxing**
"I can give you 10 minutes at [time]. Will that help?"

*When: You can help, but only briefly.*
*Tone: Helpful and specific.*
*Impact: You're saying yes, but with a clear boundary.*

**Script E10 - Setting a Pattern**
"I've made a commitment to focus time from [time] to [time]. Everything else I can help with outside those hours."

*When: You want to protect blocks of deep work.*
*Tone: Professional, matter-of-fact.*
*Impact: People learn when you're available and plan accordingly.*

## SECTION F: BOSS CONVERSATIONS (10 SCRIPTS)

### When You Need to Push Back to Your Manager About Priorities or Workload

**Script F1 - Alignment Request**
"I want to make sure I'm focused on what matters most to you. Can we align on priorities?"

*When: You need to understand what your boss actually cares about.*
*Tone: Eager, collaborative.*
*Impact: Your boss gets to clarify. You get clarity.*

**Script F2 - The Capacity Question**
"I have three major projects. I can't do all three equally well. What should take priority?"

*When: You need them to make a decision about prioritization.*
*Tone: Honest, realistic.*
*Impact: Forces a decision that's their responsibility, not yours.*

**Script F3 - Reality Check**
"I want to set realistic expectations—given everything on my plate, here's what I can realistically deliver well: [X by date Y]."

*When: You're concerned about overpromising.*
*Tone: Professional, proactive.*
*Impact: Prevents future disappointment.*

**Script F4 - Asking for Help**
"I'm running into a capacity issue. Do you want me to push back on new work, bring in a resource, or shift something?"

*When: You're identifying the problem and offering solutions.*
*Tone: Problem-solving, not complaining.*
*Impact: You're taking responsibility for solving it.*

**Script F5 - Observing a Pattern**
"I notice the load has increased significantly. Is that intentional, or should we recalibrate?"

*When: You're concerned about sustained overload.*
*Tone: Gentle, not accusatory.*
*Impact: Gives them a chance to course-correct.*

**Script F6 - Asking for Clarity**
"I'm getting conflicting priorities from different directions. Can you help me understand what's most important right now?"

*When: People are asking for different things.*
*Tone: Seeking guidance.*
*Impact: Your boss clarifies what matters most.*

**Script F7 - With Data**
"Here's my current workload: [list]. Here's what's coming: [list]. Here's what I can realistically deliver: [list]. Where should I focus?"

*When: You want to make the situation visible.*
*Tone: Factual, organized.*
*Impact: Concrete discussion instead of vague feelings.*

**Script F8 - Offering Options**
"I see this becoming an issue. Would you rather I: (1) push back on new work, (2) extend my hours, or (3) focus only on high-priority items?"

*When: You want them to choose the trade-off.*
*Tone: Solution-focused.*
*Impact: They understand what's sustainable and what's not.*

**Script F9 - Respecting Their Constraints**
"I know you're dealing with pressure from above. I'm not trying to push back on the work—I want to make sure we're realistic about timeline."

*When: You understand they're under pressure too.*
*Tone: Empathetic, collaborative.*
*Impact: Shows you're thinking about their constraints, not just yours.*

**Script F10 - The Direct Conversation**
"I need your help. The current workload is unsustainable. What can we do to fix this?"

*When: It's serious and needs a real conversation.*
*Tone: Serious, honest.*
*Impact: Opens a real dialogue about a real problem.*

## HOW TO USE THIS PACK

**For Each Situation:**
1. Read the script that feels closest to your situation
2. Adapt it to your language (use these as templates)
3. Practice saying it out loud
4. Notice the tone and adjust if needed
5. Use it in the actual conversation

**Pick One Script at a Time**
Don't try to memorize all 75. Pick the ones that fit your most common situations. Master those first. Add others as you need them.

**It's Normal to:**
* Adapt these to your voice
* Combine scripts
* Make them shorter or longer
* Adjust the language

**It's Not:**
* Script memorization
* Robotic delivery
* Exactly how you'd say it

These are starting points. Make them yours.
